Matchmaker Comparison

Simulations compared: random, roundrobin, stochastic

Match Throughput per Bot

Each dot is one bot over the full simulation. X: total matches played. Y: average game duration. A narrow horizontal cluster within one matchmaker means matches are distributed evenly across bots — a wide spread indicates some bots are being dispatched more often than others. Differences in Y between matchmakers would hint at selection bias (e.g., a matchmaker preferentially picking a bot's short-game matchups).

Skill Gap of Matched Pairs

Distribution of |ELOa − ELOb| at dispatch time (last 500 matches per bot). A skill-matched matchmaker concentrates mass near zero; a random or bucket-based matchmaker has a flatter, wider distribution. Computed over the late-simulation window only, so this is not diluted by the warm-up period when all ELOs start near 1600.

Opponent Concentration

For each bot, sort its opponents by games played (most first) and track the cumulative share of the bot's matches (last 500 matches per bot). Each curve is the median across bots within that matchmaker; the shaded band is the interquartile range. The dashed diagonal is perfect equality (bot plays every opponent equally often). Curves close to the diagonal = even spread across opponents; curves bowed up toward the top-left = a few opponents dominate that bot's schedule.

Mean vs Max Matches per Opponent

Each dot is one bot (last 500 matches per bot). X: mean number of matches against each of its opponents. Y: games played against its most-frequent opponent. The ratio Y / X is the per-bot concentration at the top opponent — equivalent to the first-step value of the curve above, but resolved per bot so outliers (hover to see names) are identifiable.

Matchup Frequency — random

How often each pair of bots played in the post-settlement window (matches 4000 onward — first 20% of the run dropped as warm-up). Rows and columns are sorted by final ELO (strongest top/left). A dense diagonal band = skill-matched pairs; a uniform color = everyone plays everyone; bright spots off-diagonal = forced matchups across the skill gap.

ELO Ladder Spread Over Time

Standard deviation of ELO across all bots at each snapshot. Starts at 0 (all bots seeded at 1600) and rises as strong/weak bots diverge; the plateau = the ladder's equilibrium spread. A higher plateau means the matchmaker produced clearer skill separation. Note: this is a population-level signal — it doesn't tell you whether individual bot rankings are stable (see the next plot for that).

Per-Bot ELO Stabilization

For each bot, its “settled ELO” is defined as its mean ELO over the second half of the snapshots. At every earlier snapshot, we compute the RMS distance from that settled value across all bots. The curve drops toward a noise floor as bots approach their long-run ratings. Read this to answer “how many matches until ELOs stop moving meaningfully?”
